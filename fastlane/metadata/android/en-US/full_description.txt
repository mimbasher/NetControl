NetControl cuts any app off both WiFi and mobile data on an unrooted phone.

Android has no built in way to do this. Samsung's "Allowed networks for apps"
lets you disable WiFi or mobile data for an app, never both. Every other no root
firewall works by declaring itself a VPN, which occupies your one and only VPN
slot and costs battery. NetControl does neither.

It uses Shizuku to borrow shell (ADB) privileges just long enough to run
Android's own firewall commands, setting the app's deny bit in
FIREWALL_CHAIN_OEM_DENY_3, a kernel BPF firewall map owned by netd. The block is
enforced below the app layer, so it covers WiFi and cellular identically and
costs no battery.

The rule lives in the kernel, not in NetControl and not in Shizuku. You only
need Shizuku at the moment you change a block. Apply your blocks, then stop
Shizuku. The blocks stay active with nothing running in the background.

Every write is read straight back from the kernel, so a rule that did not stick
says so rather than showing a green switch and lying to you. The header counts
anything that is not enforced, so a silent failure cannot hide.

A reboot clears the map. NetControl re-applies your saved blocks automatically
the next time you open it with Shizuku available.

Requires Android 15 or newer. The commands this relies on do not exist on
Android 14 and older.

This F-Droid build declares no INTERNET permission at all. It cannot phone home
because it cannot reach a network.
